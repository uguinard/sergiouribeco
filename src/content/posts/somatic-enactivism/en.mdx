The traditional classroom often treats the mind as a disembodied hard drive, passively receiving data. Somatic enactivism challenges this by positioning language acquisition as a fundamentally physical act. 

When we engage the nervous system through physical movement and spatial awareness, linguistic structures bind to somatic memory. This requires moving beyond static texts and embracing [[The Architecture of Natural Induction]]. 

By designing environments where meaning is negotiated through physical interaction, we create a deeper, more resilient neurological pathway. This approach pairs exceptionally well with [[Comprehensible Input Strategies]], ensuring the input is not just heard, but physically felt.
